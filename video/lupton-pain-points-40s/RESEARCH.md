# Buyer Pain-Point Research — Lupton 40s Video (Aug 2026)

Research target: OEM buyers, sourcing engineers, and procurement managers who buy
custom electro-mechanical components — the exact people Lupton's reps call on.
Method: web + forum/Reddit-ecosystem search for first-person complaints about
sourcing custom parts. (Reddit blocks direct crawling, so Reddit-voiced pain points
are corroborated through industry write-ups that survey those same communities.)

## Pain point 1 — The RFQ black hole (slow, inaccurate quotes)

- Fewer than **25% of rapid quotes in custom manufacturing are accurate**; inaccurate
  quoting is ranked the single top pain point in the entire custom-manufacturing
  sourcing process (Smart Industry research via SpecLens).
- Engineers "send out RFQs and wait a week for quotes that come back all over the map,"
  then burn weeks in clarification loops — overpaying or under-specifying risk.
- Different estimators at the same supplier extract different data from the same PDF
  drawing package, so quotes are inconsistent even from one shop.
- Sources: [SpecLens — quote accuracy](https://www.speclens.ai/blog/custom-manufacturing-procurement-quote-accuracy),
  [Jiga (YC) launch — messy RFQ email threads](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/MXT-jiga-the-white-box-manufacturing-platform),
  [Vendra (YC)](https://ycombinator.com/companies/vendra)

## Pain point 2 — Messy sourcing process

- Buyers juggle email threads and spreadsheets of quotes per process (sheet metal shop,
  molder, machine shop, EMS house) — miscommunication, delays, and cost creep.
- Typical 2–3 weeks just to get prototype parts after ordering.

## Pain point 3 — Lead times slip

- Supply-chain reliability is the #1 recurring headache in sourcing injection molding
  and fabrication; finding a reliable domestic partner is framed as the fix.
- Sources: [Crescent Industries](https://www.crescentind.com/blog/sourcing-injection-molding-and-combating-supply-chain-frustrations),
  [Rodon Group](https://www.rodongroup.com/blog/10-questions-to-ask-when-sourcing-an-injection-molder/)

## Pain point 4 — Offshore quality & accountability

- Ambiguous defect definitions make accountability unenforceable; opaque layers of
  subcontracting hide who actually makes the part; language/culture gaps compound it.
- Sources: [Thaco — risks of contract manufacturing](https://thacoindustries.com/en/risks-of-contract-manufacturing/),
  [E-Business International](https://e-bi.com/what-is-contract-manufacturing-a-complete-guide-for-oems/)

## Pain point 5 — Tariff volatility & China risk (2026's #1 disruptor)

- **72%** of senior trade professionals name US tariff volatility the most impactful
  regulatory change of 2026; **82%** of supply-chain leaders say tariffs affected their
  supply chains; **43%** of companies plan to shift supply chains to the US in 3 years.
- "China Plus One" has become the operational standard; China's 2026 export-control
  countermeasures add new compliance risk.
- Sources: [Ivalua](https://www.ivalua.com/blog/how-tariffs-impact-procurement-and-supply-chains/),
  [McKinsey supply-chain risk survey](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/operations/our-insights/supply-chain-risk-survey),
  [Indigrowth 2026 trends](https://indigrowth.com/supply-chain-trends-2026-tariffs-nearshoring-resilience/),
  [Pharmaceutical Commerce — China countermeasures](https://www.pharmaceuticalcommerce.com/view/how-china-turned-us-trade-compliance-into-a-supply-chain-risk)

## Pain point 6 — Single-source risk

- Tariff-driven cost shocks can make a key supplier unviable overnight, threatening
  supply continuity (KPMG supply-chain update).
- Source: [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/us/en/articles/2025/february-2025-supply-chain-update.html)

## Why this maps to Lupton

Every pain point is the inverse of Lupton's model: one call → 25+ vetted principals
across every process (sheet metal, molding, machining, PCBA, cable), domestic + Asia
options (Tongrun China/Thailand/TX for tariff flexibility), local rep engineers who
answer the phone, and 55+ years of accountability ("Servicing the Working World
Since 1969").
